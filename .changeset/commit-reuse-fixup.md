`git commit -c`, `--fixup` and `--squash` (statewalker/statewalker-root#292). `CommitCommand.setReeditMessage(commit)` is `-c`: the commit's message and authorship, as `setReuseMessage` (`-C`) takes them, given to the editor (`setEditor`; without one the message is taken as it is). `setFixup("<commit>")` makes `fixup! <subject>` (then the `-m` text), `setFixup("amend:<commit>")` makes `amend! <subject>` followed by the commit's message (its body when it is an `amend!` commit) for the editor, and `setFixup("reword:<commit>")` does the same committing HEAD's tree; `setSquash(commit)` puts `squash! <subject>` before the message (`-m`, `-C`, `-c` or the editor's). The refusals are git's, with its texts and exit codes: `-m` with `-C`/`-c` or `--fixup=amend:`/`reword:`, `-C`/`-c`/`--fixup` together, `--fixup` with `--squash`, an unknown `--fixup` suboption, a revision that names no commit or names a tree (`could not lookup commit`), `reword:` with paths, `-a`, `-i` or during a merge or cherry-pick, and an `amend!` message without a body. `--amend --fixup` keeps no author, as git. Such commits fold under `rebase -i --autosquash`, native or ours. The template (`-t`, `commit.template`) is also dropped with `-c` and `--fixup`.
