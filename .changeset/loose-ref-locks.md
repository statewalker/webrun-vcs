**packed-refs.lock is held for the whole packed-refs update, as git does.** A rewrite now writes the complete new content into `packed-refs.new`, renames that over `packed-refs` while `packed-refs.lock` is still held, lets the caller finish, and only then removes the lock (git's `refs/packed-backend.c`). A delete therefore drops the ref from `packed-refs` and removes its loose file in one pass under the lock, with no re-take and re-check loop that a steady stream of `packRefs` could starve, and a multi-ref batch keeps the lock across both of its phases. Readers never see a partial `packed-refs`, and native git sees our lock until the loose file is gone. A `packed-refs.new` left by a crash is overwritten by the next rewrite and removed by `gc` (under `packed-refs.lock`, whatever its age); `FilesRefOps.sweepTemporaries` does the same on its own.

**`FileRefStore.set`, `setSymbolic` and `remove` take `<ref>.lock`.** `set` is a one-command batch without an old-value check (`noDeref`, so it still replaces a symbolic ref), so it gets the batch's D/F checks and reflog lines; `setSymbolic` writes `ref: <target>` into the lock and renames it over the ref; `remove` deletes under `<ref>.lock` and then `packed-refs.lock` in one pass, and removes the ref's reflog as git does. A lock held past `refLockTimeoutMs` (100 ms) makes them throw the new `RefUpdateRejectedError` (`code` "ELOCKED") with the ref unchanged; a D/F conflict throws it with `code` "EREJECTED", now on every FilesApi. Names outside `refs/` other than HEAD (`ORIG_HEAD`) are still accepted by `set`, written under their lock without a reflog.
